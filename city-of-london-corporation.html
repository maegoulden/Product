Problem Discovery

Women interested in digital careers face barriers that make career transitions more difficult.

The Women Pivoting to Digital Taskforce's report, Recruiting and Retaining Female Digital Talent: Insights for Employers, explored the experiences of over 1,000 women who had either transitioned into digital roles or were considering doing so. The findings informed the development of the Turn to Tech programme.

Research Findings

Many women are motivated to move into digital careers but face barriers to making the transition.

The course was informed by two reports published by the City of London Corporation's Women Pivoting to Digital Taskforce. Untapped Digital Talent: The £3bn Opportunity explored the UK's digital skills shortage, while Recruiting and Retaining Female Digital Talent: Insights for Employers surveyed 1,013 women to understand the barriers and opportunities shaping digital career transitions.

89%

Women interested in a digital career would reskill if offered the opportunity.

59%

Women who want to career change said it was unclear how to secure a digital role.

32%

Women interested in a digital career have been given the opportunity to reskill within their current organisation.

The Solution

Turn to Tech - The Digital Career Change Course

In response to the challenges identified through the Taskforce's research, the City of London Corporation developed Turn to Tech - a digital career transition programme designed to help women explore digital careers, recognise their transferable skills, build confidence and understand the practical steps towards employment.

My Role

Transforming an early concept into a tested learner experience.

I joined the project after the research, course content and initial concept had already been developed. My role was to review the existing prototype and design the learner experience: the journey, course flow, and interactive activities, presented clearly and accessibly. Shortly after I joined, the brief shifted to a PDF as the MVP so I designed the learner experience for that format instead. As new sections were developed, they were evaluated through usability testing with 15 participants, allowing feedback to shape ongoing iterations throughout the design process.

The Pivot

COLC changed the brief to a PDF just after I joined, I kept believing the website was the better long-term fit.

The PDF was the right call for a first version: faster to produce, easier to circulate to partners like Nationwide and Lloyds, and lower-risk if sections needed to change. I delivered it to that brief. But de-risking the release doesn't solve the problem long-term, so I kept building the website version alongside it, on my own initiative.

My Initiative

A PDF couldn't reach the group who needed it most: The Curious.

The Curious are women unsure digital is "for them". The group least likely to open a static document and most in need of something that feels inviting before they've committed to anything. I did competitive research into how other reskilling programmes handle that same first step, and used it alongside the Taskforce's own findings to build the case for a website.

User group How well does the PDF serve them? Priority for the website
The Curious Poorly. A PDF asks for commitment before they're convinced there's anything in it for them Highest. The group a PDF structurally can't reach
The Career Changer Reasonably. Content works, but no structure or sense of progress Medium
The Returner Fine. Mainly needs flexible pacing, which either format can offer Lower

The Trade-Off

Ship fast and safe, or build for reach and iteration.

This wasn't "website beats PDF", both sides had a real case, and I tried to weigh them honestly rather than just advocate for the option I preferred.

Ship the PDF Build the website
Faster, cheaper to change Testable and instrumented
No buy-in needed before content is proven Can measure conversion by user group
Lower risk for a first release Higher upfront investment

Rather than pick a side, I built the second option far enough that COLC could make the call with something real in front of them.

Where It Stands

The PDF is ready with 500+ pre-launch sign-ups; the website is built and ready to pitch.

The PDF has done what an MVP should before launch: validate the content and the demand without over-investing before either was proven. The website is built as the proposal for what comes next, grounded in the same research and testing, plus the competitive research I did to make the case for going digital.

Reflection

Delivering to the brief doesn't mean the job stops there.

The easy version of this project is "I designed a course." What I learned more from was deciding what to do when the brief changed, respecting COLC's call while still building the case for the format I believed in. That's the instinct I want in a product role: not overriding a decision, but not stopping at "that's the brief" either.
