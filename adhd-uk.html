ADHD UK

Making ADHD support clearer, more accessible and easier to navigate

I was sat at my desk, trying to find information on ADHD in schools. Fifteen minutes and eleven tabs later, I closed the site and found what I needed somewhere else. I have ADHD. So did the students I was looking this up for. So did most of the people that site was written for. It had defeated all of us.

Problem Discovery

People came to ADHD UK for support, but the experience made it difficult to find it.

Through desk research, user interviews, and usability testing, three key usability challenges emerged.

Information overload

Dense content and excessive information made pages overwhelming and difficult to process for users.

Confusing navigation

Users struggle to understand where to go or how to find the information they needed.

An unwelcoming experience

Outdated visuals, dense layouts, and inconsistent design made the website feel unwelcoming.

How Might We?

Create an ADHD-friendly website that makes finding trusted information and support simple, accessible and intuitive?

Jump to solution

Desk Research

Researching ADHD established the foundations for an accessible, ADHD-friendly redesign.

I explored ADHD research, cognitive load and accessibility guidelines to understand how digital experiences can better support neurodivergent users. The research revealed how challenges with attention, organisation and information processing translate into specific design needs, informing decisions focused on clarity, accessibility and reducing cognitive load.

Current Website Evaluation

Evaluating the existing experience exposed key usability issues

Reviewing the website identified issues with navigation, content organisation, readability and visual hierarchy, helping establish areas for improvement before conducting user research.

Competitive Audit

Competitive audit uncovered opportunities to create a clearer and more accessible experience.

I benchmarked direct and comparable non-profit websites against the existing ADHD UK website to identify strengths, weaknesses and opportunities for improvement.

Information Architecture

Restructuring the navigation established clearer routes to support.

I mapped the existing navigation to identify duplicate content, overlapping categories and multiple points of entry. Using card sorting, I reorganised information into clearer user-focused categories before creating an updated sitemap that reduced complexity and improved discoverability.

Usability Testing

Unmoderated usability tests informed iterations to the designs.

Usability testing was conducted with six participants. Participants completed three task-based scenarios, followed by targeted questions to explore their experiences. Findings were analysed to identify key patterns to inform iterations. This process led to improvements and refinements to both the low-fidelity and high-fidelity prototypes.

1. Clarified Navigation labels

  • Users mistook "About" for information about ADHD rather than the organisation
  • Renaming it to "About Us" clarified the navigation and improved usability

The Solution — Redesign

A clearer, ADHD-friendly website with a simpler, more accessible experience.

Clearer Navigation

Guided navigation helps users clearly find information and support

  • Organises content into intuitive categories to reduce confusion and cognitive load.
  • Removes duplicate pages and multiple points of entry.
  • Reduced options to help users reach information faster.

Structured Content

Well-structured content makes complex information easier to understand.

  • Breaks down complex information into manageable sections, reducing cognitive overload.
  • Improved visual hierarchy, consistent layouts reduce reading fatigue.

Easier Access to Support

A redesigned homepage guides users to the information they need faster.

  • Prioritises the most common user journeys directly from the homepage.
  • Reduces decision fatigue with clear calls-to-action for key tasks.
  • Guides users to relevant support based on their individual needs.
